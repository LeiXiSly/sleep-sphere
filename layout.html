THE PROBLEM

Why draw sleep
at all

Sleep is the hardest thing to observe in yourself. You remember when you lay down last night, but not what has been stealing your deep sleep across thirty days.

A table can record it, but it cannot let you see it. Once a month is arranged into a sphere, which day collapsed, which line pulls which — the shape tells the truth for you.

Thirty days of numbers do not turn into insight on their own. They need to be arranged into a form you can look at, rotate, and walk into — then the question becomes concrete.

So this tool does one thing only: turn abstract, subjective feeling into a structure the eye can read.

THREE LAYERS

Points, lines, planes

Point · one day

The higher the score, the further from the core and the brighter. Where it collapses, you see it at once.

Line · two days linked

Days next to each other, or sharing the same event — both had coffee, both were grinding — that makes a line.

Plane · one habit

Three lines enclose an area. When the same tag keeps showing up, it grows into a plane on its own.

Logged dates spiral down the sphere, from the pole at the start of the month to the pole at the end. After thirty days, what you see is not a table — it is a person's architecture.
